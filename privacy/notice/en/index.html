1. Reason for the amendment

The amendment aligns the wording of the policy with what the Company actually processes. No new items of personal information are collected, and no purpose of use is added. If anything, the range of information the Company itself holds becomes narrower.

2. What changes

(1) How the website is described (top of the document)

Before — Website (jinazen.com): the Company collects and stores personal information.
After — Website (jinazen.com): the Company processes only what an enquirer chooses to send.

The website has no membership registration and no payment, and the only processing the Company carries out is enquiries and grievance handling (Article 1). The summary at the top of the document read differently from Article 1, and has been corrected.

(2) Access logs — Articles 2 and 3

Before — Article 2(2): Automatically generated — IP address, access date and time, and other service access logs
Article 3: Access logs: 3 months
After — Article 2(2): Access logs — the Company does not itself collect or store website access logs. The hosting provider (Cloudflare, Inc.) may process access IP addresses and similar data in the course of providing the service; Article 5 governs that processing.
Article 3: the "Access logs: 3 months" entry is removed.

The Company does not receive the website's server logs. Handling of access logs is a matter of the hosting arrangement, so it is consolidated into the outsourcing and overseas-transfer entries of Article 5. Previously, the same data was given two different retention periods — "3 months" in Article 3 and "until the outsourcing contract ends" in Article 5(4).

(3) Cookies — Article 6

Before — The Company does not use cookies on the website.
After — The Company uses no cookies for targeted advertising or web analytics. The hosting provider may, however, set cookies for the purpose of service security; the Company neither reads nor uses those cookies.

It remains the case that the Company uses no advertising or analytics cookies and no user-tracking tools. However, the hosting provider may set cookies for service security, so the earlier phrase "does not use cookies at all" could have been inaccurate, and has been restated precisely.
